Frequently asked questions

How much of a raise from £80,000 to £90,000 do I actually keep in the UK?

A £10,000 gross raise from £80,000 to £90,000 adds about £5,800 to your annual take-home pay in the UK for the 2025/26 tax year — roughly 58% of the raise, after Income Tax and National Insurance.

What is the marginal tax rate on this raise?

Across the £80,000 to £90,000 slice, the effective marginal deduction rate is about 42.0%. That means for every extra £100 of gross pay in this band you keep roughly £58 after Income Tax and National Insurance.

How much more will I take home each month?

Your monthly take-home pay rises from about £4,746 at £80,000 to £5,230 at £90,000 — an extra £483 a month in the UK for the 2025/26 tax year.

About this comparison

Figures use the 2025/26 tax year and the same logic as our UK Salary Calculator: the £12,570 personal allowance (tapered above £100,000), Income Tax at 20% / 40% / 45% and Class 1 National Insurance at 8% then 2%. The "net raise" is simply the take-home pay at £90,000 minus the take-home pay at £80,000. These are estimates for the 2025/26 tax year and are not personal tax advice.
