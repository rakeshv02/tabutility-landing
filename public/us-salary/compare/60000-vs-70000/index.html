Frequently asked questions

How much of a raise from $60,000 to $70,000 do I actually keep in the US?

A $10,000 gross raise from $60,000 to $70,000 adds about $7,685 to your annual take-home pay in the US for the 2026 tax year — roughly 77% of the raise, after federal tax, Social Security and Medicare.

What is the marginal tax rate on this raise?

Across the $60,000 to $70,000 slice, the effective marginal deduction rate is about 23.2%. That means for every extra $100 of gross pay in this band you keep roughly $77 after federal tax, Social Security and Medicare.

How much more will I take home each month?

Your monthly take-home pay rises from about $4,199 at $60,000 to $4,840 at $70,000 — an extra $640 a month in the US for the 2026 tax year.

About this comparison

Figures use the 2026 tax year and the same logic as our US Paycheck Calculator: a single filer in Texas (no state income tax), federal income tax on the 2026 brackets, Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%, plus 0.9% above $200,000). The "net raise" is simply the take-home pay at $70,000 minus the take-home pay at $60,000. These are estimates for the 2026 tax year and are not personal tax advice.
