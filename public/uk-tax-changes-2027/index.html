UK Tax Changes 2027

Every change confirmed for the 2027/28 tax year (starting 6 April 2027), based only on official GOV.UK and HMRC announcements — no speculation. Last reviewed 8 August 2026.

The headline: savings and property income taxed harder from April 2027

At Budget 2025 (26 November 2025), the Chancellor announced a 2-percentage-point rise in tax on income from assets, phased in over two years. Dividends went up first in April 2026; savings and property income follow in April 2027. Tax rates on wages and self-employment profits are not changing — but the freeze on thresholds continues, which quietly raises the tax on pay rises.

1. Savings income: +2pp from 6 April 2027 Announced at Budget 2025

Band2026/27 rateFrom April 2027
Basic rate20%22%
Higher rate40%42%
Additional rate45%47%

The personal savings allowance (£1,000 basic rate / £500 higher rate) and the £5,000 starting rate for savings were not abolished in the announcement, so most people with modest savings interest will still pay nothing. The higher rates bite once interest exceeds your allowances — one more reason ISAs get more valuable from 2027.

Source: HMRC tax information and impact note — Changes to tax rates for property, savings and dividend income (GOV.UK)

2. Property income: new separate rates from 6 April 2027 Announced at Budget 2025

For the first time, rental income gets its own income tax rates — 2 percentage points above the rates on wages:

BandRate on wagesNew property rate (April 2027)
Property basic rate20%22%
Property higher rate40%42%
Property additional rate45%47%

Mortgage interest (finance cost) relief will be given at the property basic rate of 22% rather than 20%, partially softening the blow for leveraged landlords. Reporting stays the same — only the rates change. A basic-rate landlord with £10,000 of taxable rental profit pays about £200/year more.

Source: HM Treasury / HMRC policy paper (GOV.UK)

4. Thresholds stay frozen — the stealth tax continues Confirmed

Government policy papers confirm the personal allowance and basic rate limit are fixed at these levels through at least 5 April 2028. With wage growth, every pay rise drags more income into the 40% and 45% bands even though headline rates haven't moved — HMRC's quietest tax rise.

Sources: GOV.UK — allowance and thresholds to 5 April 2028 · GOV.UK — rates and thresholds for employers 2026/27

What has NOT been announced (as of August 2026)

Full 2027/28 payroll thresholds (National Insurance limits, Scottish bands) haven't been published yet — HMRC typically releases the employer rates tables in January/February 2027. No changes to income tax rates on wages, employee NI rates, or the pension annual allowance have been announced for April 2027. FAQs

What tax changes take effect in the UK in April 2027?

Savings income tax rises 2 percentage points (22%/42%/47%) and property income gets separate rates of 22%/42%/47%, with finance cost relief at 22%. Both were announced at Budget 2025.

Is income tax on wages going up in 2027?

No rate rise on employment income has been announced. But frozen thresholds mean fiscal drag continues to raise effective tax bills as pay rises.

What happened to dividend tax?

It rose 2pp a year earlier, from April 2026: ordinary rate 10.75%, upper rate 35.75%. The additional rate stays 39.35%.

How much more will landlords pay from April 2027?

Rental profits move to 22%/42%/47% rates — roughly £200/year more per £10,000 of profit for a basic-rate landlord, partly offset by mortgage relief at 22% instead of 20%.

Are thresholds still frozen in 2027/28?

Yes — £12,570 personal allowance and £37,700 basic rate limit are confirmed frozen through at least 5 April 2028.
