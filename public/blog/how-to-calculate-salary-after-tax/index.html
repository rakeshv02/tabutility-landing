How to Calculate Your Salary After Tax:
UK & US Guide

8 min read · Tax · Updated July 2026

Your gross salary is the number in your job offer. Your take-home pay — or "net salary" — is what actually lands in your bank account. The gap between the two surprises most people, and understanding exactly where it goes helps you budget accurately and find legal ways to keep more.

UK Salary After Tax: How It Works

UK employees pay two main deductions: Income Tax and National Insurance contributions (NICs). Your employer also deducts these via PAYE (Pay As You Earn) before paying you.

UK Example: £45,000 Salary

Gross Salary: £45,000

Income Tax:
· £0–£12,570 = £0 (personal allowance)
· £12,571–£45,000 = £32,430 × 20% = £6,486
Total tax: £6,486

National Insurance:
· £12,571–£45,000 = £32,430 × 8% = £2,594

Take-home pay: £45,000 − £6,486 − £2,594 = £35,920/year (£2,993/month)

Things That Reduce Your UK Tax Bill

  • Pension contributions — contributions to a workplace pension reduce your taxable income. At 40% tax, a £100 pension contribution costs you only £60.
  • Gift Aid donations — charities can claim 25p per £1, and higher-rate taxpayers can claim additional relief.
  • Salary sacrifice — trading salary for benefits (childcare vouchers, cycle-to-work, EV cars) reduces your NIC and tax liability.
  • Marriage Allowance — if one partner earns below the personal allowance, they can transfer up to £1,260 of their allowance to the higher earner.
💡 The £100,000 trap: Earning £100,001 costs you your personal allowance — the effective marginal tax rate on earnings between £100,000 and £125,140 is 60%. Pension contributions are the most powerful tool to avoid this.

Your Effective Tax Rate vs Marginal Rate

People often confuse these two. Your marginal rate is the tax rate on your last pound/dollar of income. Your effective rate is the total tax you pay divided by your total income.

A UK earner on £80,000 is a 40% higher-rate taxpayer (marginal rate), but their effective income tax rate is closer to 23% — because only the portion above £50,270 is taxed at 40%.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the take-home pay on a £30,000 salary in the UK?

On £30,000 in 2026/27, you'd pay approximately £3,486 in income tax and £1,394 in National Insurance, giving a take-home of around £25,120/year or £2,093/month.

What is the take-home pay on a £50,000 salary in the UK?

On £50,000 you'd pay approximately £7,486 in income tax and £3,002 in National Insurance, giving a take-home of around £39,512/year or £3,293/month.

How much is £100,000 after tax in the UK?

At £100,000, you begin losing your personal allowance. Expect to pay approximately £27,432 in income tax and £5,338 in National Insurance, giving a take-home of approximately £67,230/year — an effective rate of about 33%.

Does pension salary sacrifice reduce National Insurance?

Yes — unlike employee pension contributions, salary sacrifice reduces your gross pay before NIC is calculated. This means you save both income tax and National Insurance on the sacrificed amount, making it more tax-efficient than standard pension contributions.
