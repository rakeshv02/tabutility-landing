The Salary You Need to Buy a Typical Home in Every US State (2026 Data)

Published 13 August 2026 · Original data analysis · Source: Zillow Home Value Index, June 2026

How much do you need to earn to buy a typical home where you live? We took the latest Zillow Home Value Index (June 2026) for all 50 states plus DC and computed the household income required, using the classic lender math: 10% down, a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, and the 28% rule (housing payment ≤ 28% of gross income).

The spread is staggering: $44,543 in West Virginia versus $203,996 in Hawaii — a 4.6× gap for the same milestone. The US median household income sits near the requirement of states in the middle of our table, which is exactly why homeownership feels effortless in some states and impossible in others.

Method in one line: required salary = 12 × monthly principal-and-interest on 90% of the state's typical home value ÷ 0.28. Property taxes, insurance and HOA fees are excluded (see method notes) — real requirements run higher, especially in high-tax states.

What Jumps Out of the Data

  • The $100k wall: in 21 states and jurisdictions you now need a six-figure household income for a typical home. A generation ago that was true almost nowhere.
  • The affordable belt: West Virginia, Mississippi, Louisiana, Oklahoma and Arkansas all clear at under $56,000 — below the income of many dual-earner households anywhere in the country. Remote workers have noticed.
  • Hawaii vs West Virginia: the identical 10%-down buyer needs 4.6× the income. No other everyday purchase varies this much by geography.
  • The middle is moving: states like Arizona, Georgia and North Carolina — affordable a decade ago — now sit in the $80–100k band, squeezing out local first-time buyers.

How Far Does Your Salary Go?

Flip the question: what does your current income buy?

  • $50,000: a typical home in just 2 states — West Virginia and Mississippi (Louisiana misses by ~$3,000).
  • $75,000: opens up 18 states, mostly in the South and Midwest.
  • $100,000: 30 states are within reach — but not Colorado, Utah, Washington, or the coasts.
  • $150,000: everything except Massachusetts, California and Hawaii.

Method Notes

Home values: Zillow Home Value Index (ZHVI), all-homes smoothed seasonally-adjusted, June 2026, by state — the "typical" mid-market home, not the median sale price. Mortgage math: 10% down payment, 30-year fixed at 6.5%, standard amortization; principal and interest only. Income requirement: P&I capped at 28% of gross household income. Excludes property tax, homeowners insurance, PMI (which applies below 20% down) and HOA fees, so treat these as lower bounds. Computed by Tabutility, August 2026. FAQs

What salary do you need to buy a house in the US?

Between roughly $45,000 and $204,000 depending on the state, for a typical home with 10% down at 6.5%.

What is the 28% rule?

Keep your housing payment at or below 28% of gross monthly income — the standard lender affordability screen.

Do these numbers include property taxes and insurance?

No — principal and interest only. Add roughly 15–25% to the required salary in high-property-tax states.

Can two incomes count?

Yes — these are household income figures. Two $55,000 earners qualify where $110,000 is required.
