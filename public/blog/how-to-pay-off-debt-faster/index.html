How to Pay Off Debt Faster:
Snowball vs Avalanche Method

7 min read · Finance · Updated July 2026

Carrying debt is expensive. Interest compounds against you every month, growing what you owe faster than many people realise. The good news: there are proven, structured strategies to become debt-free faster — and the difference between a haphazard approach and a strategic one can be thousands of pounds (or dollars) and years of your life.

Two methods dominate personal finance advice: the Debt Snowball and the Debt Avalanche. Both work — but for different reasons and different people. Here's everything you need to choose the right one.

Example Debt Scenario

Let's use a real example throughout. You have four debts and a spare £500/month to put toward repayment:

Credit Card A£1,200 balance22% APR
Personal Loan£5,000 balance9% APR
Credit Card B£3,000 balance18% APR
Car Finance£8,000 balance6% APR

Total debt: £17,200. Minimum payments: ~£280/month. Extra available: £500/month. Total payment: £780/month.

Method 1: The Debt Snowball

❄️ Debt Snowball

How it works: Pay minimums on all debts. Put ALL extra money toward the smallest balance. When it's gone, roll that payment to the next smallest.

Order for our example: Credit Card A (£1,200) → Credit Card B (£3,000) → Personal Loan (£5,000) → Car Finance (£8,000)

Psychology: You clear debts completely in ~2.5 months for the first one. Each win gives you motivation to keep going. Research (including studies by the Harvard Business Review) shows that people who feel momentum are more likely to stay on track.

Method 2: The Debt Avalanche

🏔️ Debt Avalanche

How it works: Pay minimums on all debts. Put ALL extra money toward the highest interest rate. When it's gone, roll that payment to the next highest rate.

Order for our example: Credit Card A (22%) → Credit Card B (18%) → Personal Loan (9%) → Car Finance (6%)

Mathematics: In this case the order happens to be the same as snowball, but that's often not true. The avalanche eliminates the debt costing you the most first — mathematically optimal.

The real-world interest difference between snowball and avalanche is often smaller than people expect — typically 5–15% of the total interest paid. What's not small is the difference between completing a strategy and giving up on one.

The Hybrid Approach

Don't feel trapped by a binary choice. Many people use a hybrid:

  • Clear the one or two smallest debts first (snowball, for the psychological win)
  • Then switch to avalanche order for the remaining, larger debts

This gives you the momentum boost of snowball early on, then captures most of the interest savings of avalanche when the larger sums are in play.

General Strategies to Accelerate Debt Payoff

  1. Stop adding to the debt. Obvious, but critical. Paying down a credit card while continuing to use it is like bailing out a boat with a hole in it.
  2. Consider a balance transfer. A 0% balance transfer card (typically available for 12–24 months) can pause interest on credit card debt, letting all your payments go directly to principal.
  3. Increase your income. A side income, overtime, or selling unused items can create extra cash to throw at debt.
  4. Reduce expenses temporarily. Even £100/month extra cuts months off your timeline and significantly reduces total interest.
  5. Automate payments. Set up direct debits for your calculated monthly payments. Removing the decision removes the temptation to spend that money instead.
  6. Use windfalls wisely. Tax refunds, bonuses, and gifts applied directly to debt can take months off your payoff date.

When Not to Aggressively Pay Down Debt

Paying off debt isn't always the best use of extra cash. Consider:

  • Emergency fund first: Without 3–6 months of living expenses saved, any unexpected cost will go straight back onto the credit card.
  • Employer pension match: If your employer matches pension contributions, this is effectively a 50–100% return. Always capture the full match before paying extra debt.
  • Low-rate debt: If your only debt is a 3% student loan or mortgage, investing may generate a higher return than the interest saved.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is better — snowball or avalanche?

Mathematically, avalanche wins. Behaviourally, snowball often wins because people stick with it longer. The best method is the one you'll actually follow to completion. If you're naturally disciplined, choose avalanche. If you need momentum and motivation, choose snowball.

How long does it take to pay off £10,000 in debt?

It depends entirely on the interest rate and how much you pay each month. At 20% APR with minimum-only payments, £10,000 could take over 20 years and cost more than £10,000 in interest. Paying £350/month would clear it in about 3.5 years at the same rate, paying roughly £4,800 in interest.

Should I pay off debt or save?

The rule of thumb: if your debt interest rate is higher than what you could realistically earn investing (historically ~7% for stocks), pay off the debt first. High-interest debt (10%+) should almost always be prioritised over savings beyond an emergency fund.

Does paying off debt improve my credit score?

Yes, in most cases. Reducing credit card utilisation (the percentage of your credit limit you're using) has a particularly large positive impact. Getting utilisation below 30% is a meaningful threshold; below 10% is optimal.
