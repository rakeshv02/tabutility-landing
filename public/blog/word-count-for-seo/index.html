Word Count for SEO:
How Long Should Your Content Be?

7 min read · Content · Updated July 2026

Content length is one of the most debated topics in SEO. "Does word count matter for rankings?" is googled thousands of times a month — and the honest answer is: directly, no; indirectly, yes. Understanding this distinction will save you from writing unnecessarily long content and help you understand why some short pages outrank long ones.

Does Google Use Word Count as a Ranking Factor?

Google has confirmed that word count is not a direct ranking signal. John Mueller, Google's Search Advocate, has stated multiple times that adding more words to a page that already fully covers a topic won't help it rank higher.

What does matter:

  • Topical completeness — does the page comprehensively answer the search query?
  • E-E-A-T — Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness
  • User signals — do readers engage with the content or bounce immediately?
  • Uniqueness — does the content add something not found elsewhere?

Longer content often ranks better because thorough coverage of a topic naturally covers more related keywords, answers more questions, and earns more backlinks — not because of the word count itself.

What Is "Thin Content"?

Google's Panda algorithm update (now part of core algorithm) targets "thin content" — pages that provide little or no value to users. Thin content isn't just about low word count; it includes:

  • Automatically generated text with no real meaning
  • Duplicate content across multiple pages
  • Pages with little text and lots of ads
  • Scraped content from other sources
  • Pages that don't answer the user's query despite being on the topic

A 200-word page that perfectly and uniquely answers a specific query can rank above a 2,000-word padded article. A 300-word product description that covers all key features is not thin. Thin content is about value, not length.

Reading Time and User Engagement

Average adult reading speed is approximately 200–250 words per minute. A 1,500-word article takes about 6–8 minutes to read — which is an excellent average session duration signal for Google.

💡 Estimated reading times:
  • 500 words → 2–3 min read
  • 1,000 words → 4–5 min read
  • 1,500 words → 6–8 min read
  • 2,500 words → 10–12 min read
  • 5,000 words → 20–25 min read

Word Count vs Content Depth: What Really Matters

The question to ask isn't "How many words should I write?" but "Have I fully answered what someone searching for this wants to know?"

To audit content depth, ask:

  1. Does this answer the main question the searcher has?
  2. Does it cover the most common follow-up questions?
  3. Is there anything a competitor covers that I haven't?
  4. Is there unique data, experience, or insight I can add that doesn't exist elsewhere?
⚠️ Avoid: Padding content with repetitive sentences, unnecessary synonyms, or filler sections just to hit a word count target. Padded content increases bounce rates when readers realise they're not getting value. This signals low quality to Google — the opposite of the intended effect.

The Skyscraper Technique: Does Length Still Win?

Brian Dean's Skyscraper Technique — finding the best content on a topic and creating something more comprehensive — drove huge results in 2015–2019. In 2026, it's less reliable because:

  • Most high-volume keywords are now served by highly authoritative sites with existing long-form content
  • Google's algorithms better understand content quality, not just coverage
  • AI-generated content has flooded the web with lengthy but low-value articles

The better modern approach: create the most helpful piece, not necessarily the longest. Unique data, expert quotes, original research, and interactive elements (like a calculator) add more value than extra word count.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 500 words enough for SEO?

Yes — for some queries. A product page, news article, or FAQ answer can rank with 500 words if it fully satisfies the user's intent. For competitive informational queries ("how to calculate compound interest"), 1,500–2,500 words will typically be needed to compete.

Does Google penalise long articles?

No — Google doesn't penalise based on length. But articles padded with repetitive or low-value content signal poor quality, which can hurt rankings through reduced engagement metrics and user signals.

How do I check word count in Google Docs?

Go to Tools → Word count (or press Ctrl+Shift+C on Windows / Cmd+Shift+C on Mac). You can also enable "Display word count while typing" to see it in real time.

What is a good reading level for SEO content?

Aim for a Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level of 8–10 for most general audiences. Shorter sentences, active voice, and simple vocabulary improve both readability and engagement. Use tools like Hemingway Editor to check reading level.
