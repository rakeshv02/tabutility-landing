🍁 Your BC Salary After Tax in Every Province (2025)

You earn the same salary in British Columbia — how much would you keep if you moved? Pick a gross salary and see your real take-home pay across all 10 Canadian provinces, ranked from most to least generous.

Every province applies the same federal income tax, CPP and EI — but the provincial tax on top varies enormously. Move from BC to Alberta and you could keep thousands more per year. Stay in Quebec and you'll see a noticeably smaller paycheque. The difference isn't just about provincial rates: Ontario adds a surtax and health premium, Quebec has its own pension plan (QPP) and parental insurance (QPIP), and Manitoba's top bracket kicks in earlier than most.

All calculations below use 2025 tax rules for a single employed person with no deductions beyond the basic personal amount. The same salary is run through every province's tax system and the results are ranked so you can see at a glance what a move would cost or save you.

    Methodology

    Every figure on this page is computed in real time using the 2025 tax rules embedded directly in this page's JavaScript. We use the 2025/2026 federal and provincial parameters published by the CRA and each provincial revenue authority. The calculations cover:

    • Federal income tax — 2025 brackets with the C$16,129 basic personal amount and corresponding 15% federal credit.
    • CPP / QPP — CPP at 5.95% on earnings between C$3,500 and C$68,500 for all provinces except Quebec, which uses QPP at 6.4% on the same band.
    • EI / QPIP — EI at 1.66% up to C$63,200 insurable earnings for all provinces except Quebec. Quebec pays EI at a reduced rate (1.31% up to C$63,200) plus QPIP at 0.494% up to C$88,000.
    • Provincial income tax — each province's 2025 brackets, basic personal amount and applicable credits (surtaxes, health premiums, abatements).
    • Quebec abatement — Quebec residents receive a 16.5% reduction in basic federal income tax because Quebec runs its own programs.

    We do not model provincial tax credits beyond the basic personal amount credit, employer contributions, or voluntary deductions (RRSP, pension, union dues). Figures are estimates for general comparison — they are not personal tax advice. For a precise calculation including your specific deductions, use our Canada Income Tax Calculator.

    Frequently asked questions

    If I move from BC to Alberta, how much more do I keep?

    On a C$100,000 salary, moving from BC to Alberta typically saves C$1,500–C$2,500 per year in provincial tax. The gap grows significantly at higher incomes — above C$125,000, Alberta's advantage can exceed C$5,000/year because Alberta's top rate of 15% is far lower than BC's 20.5%.

    Does Quebec have lower take-home pay than BC?

    Yes, Quebec typically has lower take-home pay than BC at most salary levels. Quebec's provincial rates are higher (up to 25.75%). Quebec residents do benefit from the 16.5% federal abatement, which partially offsets this — but the net result is still less after-tax income than BC for most salary ranges.

    Which Canadian province has the highest take-home pay?

    Alberta consistently offers the highest take-home pay for middle and higher incomes. Alberta has no provincial surtax, no health premium, no provincial sales tax, and rates that top out at 15% (vs BC's 20.5% or Ontario's 13.16% base with surtax on top).

    Why is BC take-home different from Ontario on the same salary?

    BC and Ontario have different provincial brackets, different basic personal amounts, and different add-ons. Ontario adds a health premium (up to C$900/year) and a surtax above C$5,315 of provincial tax. BC has neither — but BC's top marginal rate of 20.5% is higher than Ontario's 13.16% base (the Ontario surtax means effective top rates are close at very high incomes).

    Does this include CPP and EI?

    Yes. CPP (5.95% on earnings between C$3,500 and C$68,500) and EI (1.66% up to C$63,200) are the same in all provinces except Quebec, which uses QPP and adds QPIP. All of these are included in every province's net pay figure.
