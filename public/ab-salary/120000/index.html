On a C$120,000 salary in Alberta, your estimated take-home pay is C$88,028 per year — roughly C$7,336 every month once federal and provincial deductions are taken out for the 2026 tax year.

This estimate is based on an Alberta resident for the full 2026 tax year. Deductions include federal income tax (14% first-bracket rate for 2026), Alberta provincial tax (8%–15%, no surtax), CPP contributions (5.95% up to $74,600 plus 4% CPP2 up to $85,000), and EI premiums (1.63% up to $68,900). Alberta has no health premium or provincial sales tax. At C$120,000 your combined effective deduction rate is about 26.6%.

Frequently asked questions

What is the take-home pay on a C$120,000 salary in Alberta?

On a C$120,000 salary, an Alberta resident takes home about C$88,028 per year, or roughly C$7,336 per month, after federal and provincial income tax, CPP and EI in 2026.

Why is Alberta take-home pay higher than Ontario on the same salary?

Alberta's provincial rates start at 8% (versus Ontario's 5.05%), but Alberta has no surtax and no health premium — the Ontario surtax and health premium of up to C$900 significantly increase the effective rate for middle and higher earners. For most salaries, Albertans keep noticeably more of their pay. At C$120,000, the difference versus Ontario is around C$-501 per year.

Does Alberta have provincial sales tax?

No. Alberta is the only province with no provincial sales tax (PST) and no harmonised sales tax (HST). Only the 5% federal GST applies to purchases, which further boosts Albertans' real purchasing power versus provinces like Ontario (13% HST) or BC (12% HST+PST).
