1
Set up Google Search Console
What agencies charge: $100–$300 one-off setup fee

Google Search Console (GSC) is a free dashboard that shows you which of your pages Google has indexed, which search queries are bringing visitors, and any technical errors Google found while crawling your site. Every website needs it. Without it you're flying blind.

How to set it up in 10 minutes:

  1. Go to search.google.com/search-console and sign in with a Google account.
  2. Add a property — choose Domain if you own the domain, or URL prefix for a subdomain or specific path.
  3. Verify ownership. The easiest method: log in to your domain registrar (GoDaddy, Namecheap, etc.) and add the DNS TXT record Google gives you. It propagates in minutes.
  4. Google starts crawling within 24–48 hours. Come back to check the Coverage report for errors and the Performance report for search queries.
✅ Free — no tool needed

This step is done entirely in Google's own interface. No third-party tool required.

2
Optimise your title tags & meta descriptions
What agencies charge: $200–$800/month ongoing

Your title tag is the blue clickable headline in Google results. Your meta description is the grey paragraph beneath it. Google doesn't use the meta description as a ranking signal, but a well-written one improves your click-through rate — and click-through rate is a ranking signal.

The rules:

  • Title tag: aim for 50–60 characters. Google truncates longer titles. Include your primary keyword near the front.
  • Meta description: 140–160 characters. Write it like ad copy — answer the searcher's question and include a subtle call to action.
  • Every page on your site should have a unique title and description. Duplicate titles send a weak signal to Google.
  • Use the SERP Snippet Preview to see exactly how your title and description will render on desktop and mobile before publishing.

The Meta Tags Generator outputs the complete <head> block — title, description, Open Graph tags for social sharing, and Twitter Card tags — all in one go. Copy and paste into your page's HTML.

3
Add schema markup (structured data)
What agencies charge: $300–$1,000 one-off

Schema markup is JSON-LD code you add inside the <head> of your page. It tells Google exactly what the page is — a FAQ, a product, a local business, an article, a how-to guide. Google uses it to display rich results: FAQ dropdowns, star ratings, event dates, recipe cards.

Rich results typically see a 20–30% higher click-through rate than plain blue links. Adding schema is one of the highest-ROI SEO tasks for the time invested.

Common schema types you should add:

  • FAQPage — for any page with a Q&A section. Google shows the questions as expandable dropdowns directly in search results.
  • Article — for blog posts and news articles. Enables the Top Stories carousel for news publishers.
  • LocalBusiness — for physical businesses. Surfaces your address, phone, and hours in the knowledge panel.
  • Product — for e-commerce pages. Enables star ratings, price, and availability in results.
  • HowTo — for step-by-step guides like this one. Google can show the steps directly in search results.
  • BreadcrumbList — shows your site structure (Home › Category › Page) under the URL in results.

Generate the JSON-LD, validate it in Google's Rich Results Test, then paste it inside a <script type="application/ld+json"> tag in your page's <head>.

4
Check keyword density & content readability
What agencies charge: $500–$2,000/month for content analysis

Keyword density is the percentage of times your target keyword appears relative to the total word count of your page. There's no magic number — Google penalises obvious "stuffing" — but if your target keyword doesn't appear naturally in your copy, headers, and alt text, Google has no signal to rank you for it.

A healthy keyword density is roughly 1–2% for the primary keyword. The Keyword Density Checker shows you the density for every phrase on your page, highlights over-used terms, and gives you a readability score (Flesch–Kincaid). Pages written at a reading age of 9–12 tend to rank better because they're accessible to more searchers.

What to check:

  • Does your primary keyword appear in the title, first paragraph, at least one H2, and the meta description?
  • Are related terms (synonyms, questions) used naturally throughout?
  • Is the reading level appropriate for your audience? Jargon-heavy text loses casual searchers.
  • Is the content long enough? Google's top results for competitive keywords average over 1,400 words. Use the Word Counter to track length as you write.

5
Set up hreflang for international pages
What agencies charge: $1,000–$3,000 for international SEO setup

If your site has the same content in more than one language, or targets more than one country (a UK version and a US version, for example), you need hreflang tags. Without them, Google may treat your pages as duplicates, split their ranking power, and show the wrong version to the wrong visitor.

Hreflang is added as HTML link elements in each page's <head>, or as XML sitemap alternates. Every page must link to every other language/region version, including itself — and each target page must return the link back. A one-directional link is ignored by Google.

When you need hreflang:

  • You run an English site for the UK (en-gb) and one for the US (en-us)
  • You have translated pages in multiple languages
  • You have country-specific subdomains (uk.example.com, us.example.com)

If you're a single-country, single-language site, skip this step entirely. Hreflang on a monolingual site adds no value.

6
Create and submit your XML sitemap
What agencies charge: $100–$500 for sitemap creation and submission

An XML sitemap is a file that lists every URL on your site you want Google to index. It doesn't guarantee Google will index those pages — that depends on quality and authority — but it tells the crawler where to look and when pages were last updated.

How to create and submit a sitemap:

  1. Generate your sitemap. Most CMS platforms (WordPress, Shopify, Wix, Squarespace) generate a sitemap automatically. For WordPress, the Yoast SEO plugin or Rank Math produces one at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml. For a static site, tools like xml-sitemaps.com can crawl and generate one for free.
  2. Verify the sitemap. Open your sitemap URL in a browser and check it loads. Every URL should return a 200 status when clicked.
  3. Submit to Google Search Console. In GSC, go to Sitemaps in the left menu, paste in your sitemap URL, and hit Submit. Google shows the number of URLs submitted vs. indexed.
  4. Submit to Bing. Log in to Bing Webmaster Tools and submit your sitemap under Sitemaps. Bing has around 6% of desktop search market share — worth two minutes of your time.
  5. Check for errors. GSC's Coverage report shows any URLs Google tried to crawl but couldn't index, with reasons (blocked by robots.txt, 404, canonical mismatch, etc.).
✅ Free — no tool needed

Done through Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, both free. Your CMS likely generates the sitemap automatically.

7
Build your internal link structure
What agencies charge: $500–$1,500 for an internal link audit

Internal links are links from one page on your site to another. They do two things: they pass "link equity" (ranking power) from strong pages to weaker ones, and they help Google understand the relationship between your content. A site with clear internal linking tends to rank better across all its pages, not just the homepage.

Internal linking best practices:

  • Every important page should have at least 3–5 internal links pointing to it from other pages on your site. Use your GSC Performance report to find your highest-traffic pages, then make sure those pages link to your important-but-weaker pages.
  • Use descriptive anchor text. "Click here" tells Google nothing. "UK income tax calculator" tells Google exactly what the target page is about. Be specific and natural — don't stuff keywords into every anchor.
  • Fix orphan pages. A page with no internal links pointing to it is an orphan — Google may not find it, and it'll never rank. Audit your site and make sure every page is reachable from at least one other page.
  • Link from body content, not just navigation. In-content links carry more weight than sidebar or footer links. When you write a new article and mention a topic you've already covered, link to it.
  • Avoid over-linking. Dozens of links on a single page dilute the value of each one. Prioritise the most useful links for the reader.

How to audit your internal links for free: Use Screaming Frog's SEO Spider (free up to 500 URLs). Crawl your site and export the Internal report. Sort by Inlinks to find orphans, and sort by Outlinks to find pages that link to nowhere.

✅ Free — no paid tool needed

Screaming Frog is free up to 500 URLs. For larger sites, Google Search Console's Links report shows your most-linked pages.

📋 Frequently asked questions

Can I really do SEO without an agency? Yes. The core tasks — meta tags, schema, keyword analysis, hreflang, sitemap — are well-documented and the tooling is free. Agencies earn their fees on strategy, competitive research, link building, and ongoing content production. The technical foundation in this checklist you can absolutely do yourself.

How long until I see results? Technical fixes (meta tags, schema, sitemap) can improve your click-through rate or unlock rich results within days to weeks of Google re-crawling. Ranking gains from content improvements take two to six months. Consistency matters more than speed.

What if I get stuck? Google's own Search Central documentation is comprehensive, free, and authoritative. The Google Search Central Community answers specific questions. Skip the SEO forums that sell courses — the official documentation is better.
