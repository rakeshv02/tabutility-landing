DIY SEO: Do It Yourself, Free

SEO agencies charge $1,000–5,000 a month. Most of that work is a checklist you can run yourself in a few hours. Here is every step, in plain English, with a free tool for each one — because the internet should be free.

1

Set up Google Search Console Free from Google

This is the dashboard agencies screenshot for their monthly reports. It shows which searches bring people to your site, which pages rank, and any indexing problems — straight from Google.

Go to search.google.com/search-console, add your domain, and verify it (usually one DNS record — your domain registrar's help pages cover it). Check it once a week. Look at the Performance tab: the queries where you rank position 5–20 are your biggest opportunities — improving those pages moves the needle fastest.

2

See how you look in Google — and fix your snippet

Your title and description are your ad in the search results. If the title gets cut off or the description is auto-generated mush, fewer people click — even when you rank well. Agencies bill "on-page optimization" for exactly this.

Paste your page's title and description into the preview tool and check they fit within Google's pixel limits, lead with the keyword, and give someone a reason to click.

3

Get your meta tags right on every page

Beyond title and description, every page should have a canonical URL, Open Graph tags (so links look good when shared on social media), and Twitter card tags. Missing or duplicated tags are the most common finding in paid audits.

Fill in the form and copy the generated, correctly-escaped HTML block into your page's <head>.

4

Add structured data (schema markup)

Structured data is machine-readable code that tells Google exactly what your page is — an article, a product, a local business, an FAQ. It can earn rich results (stars, FAQs, breadcrumbs) that make your listing bigger and more clickable. Agencies charge per page for this.

Pick your page type, fill in the fields, and paste the generated JSON-LD into your page. Then confirm it validates with Google's free Rich Results Test.

5

Check your content quality before you publish

Google rewards pages that cover a topic properly and read naturally. Two quick checks catch most problems: keyword stuffing (repeating your keyword so often it reads like spam) and poor readability (long sentences that lose readers). Content optimization is a core line item on agency retainers.

Paste your draft in and aim for natural keyword usage (roughly 1–2% density) and a readability score your audience can actually follow.

6

Serve multiple countries? Fix your hreflang

If you have pages for different countries or languages (a UK and a US version, say), hreflang tags tell Google which version to show to whom. They are notoriously easy to get wrong — missing return links and invalid codes are among the most common errors in professional audits.

Generate a correct tag set for your language/country pairs, add it to your pages, then run the checker on your live URLs to confirm nothing is missing or broken. Single-country sites can skip this step.

7

Submit a sitemap and keep it current Free

A sitemap is a simple file listing every page you want Google to index. Most website builders (WordPress, Shopify, Wix, Squarespace) generate one automatically at /sitemap.xml.

In Search Console, open Sitemaps and submit yours once — Google re-checks it automatically. When you publish something new, make sure it appears in the sitemap and link to it from an existing page; internal links are how Google (and visitors) discover new content.

8

Earn links — the honest version

Links from other websites are Google's biggest trust signal, and link building is the one service where agencies genuinely sweat. You can do the honest version yourself: publish something genuinely useful or interesting (original data, a free tool, a definitive guide), then tell the people who'd care — relevant communities, newsletters, journalists covering your niche.

Avoid buying links or link "packages" — Google penalizes schemes, and cheap links are the fastest way to turn an SEO budget into a ranking problem.

9

Review monthly, improve the winners

This is the "reporting" part of the retainer. Once a month, open Search Console and ask two questions: which pages are gaining impressions, and which queries rank just off the first page? Improve those pages first — expand the content, sharpen the title, add schema.

Feeding your winners beats spreading effort evenly. It's the same prioritization logic agencies sell, and it takes about an hour a month.

FAQs

Can I really do SEO myself without an agency?

Yes — for most small websites, the bulk of what an agency delivers is technical hygiene, content improvement, and monitoring, all doable yourself with free tools and a few hours a month. Agencies add the most value on large sites, very competitive industries, and link-building campaigns.

How much do SEO agencies charge?

Typical retainers run $1,000–5,000/month for small-to-medium businesses, one-off audits $500–2,000, and content $100–500 per article. Much of the underlying work uses the same checks available in the free tools above.

How long does DIY SEO take to show results?

Technical fixes can be re-crawled within days to weeks. Ranking improvements from new content typically take 2–6 months. SEO compounds — the work keeps paying off.

What free tools do I need?

Google Search Console for monitoring, plus the on-page utilities on this page: snippet preview, meta tags, schema, keyword density & readability, and hreflang tools if you serve multiple countries.

What parts of SEO are hard to do yourself?

Link building is the most labor-intensive part, and competitive keyword research at scale is harder solo. But most sites lose rankings to missing basics, not missing backlinks — get steps 1–7 right first.
